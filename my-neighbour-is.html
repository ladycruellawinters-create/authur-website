Welcome to Cedar Hollow. An idyllic town with extraordinary residents: vampires, werewolves, and witches call this place home. For newcomer Oliver Hart, inheriting his great-aunt Agnes’s house is just the beginning. Discover the neighbours and mysteries that make this supernatural community so unusual.

A stroll through Cedar Hollow

Tucked away from the bustle of bigger places, Cedar Hollow has all the ingredients of a picture-perfect small town: independent shops, cafés, bakeries, and neighbours who stop for a chat. Children kick a ball along the street, flowers spill onto the pavement, and there’s always something to catch the eye.

Main Street

Among the shops is Twilight Books, with its dark green frontage and freshly painted sign. Next door, a pastel-pink florist adds a riot of colour to the street. A little further along, the pristine white dental practice Pearly Whites sits back from the shopfronts, offering its advice in neon: “Flossing is your best friend.”

A few curious sights

Not every local quite fits the postcard. A remarkably pale man in an oversized trench coat feeds a gathering of ravens from a bench—a small glimpse of Cedar Hollow’s more unusual character.

Beyond the shopfronts

A grand town hall anchors the far end of the road, while an old observatory watches over Cedar Hollow from the hill behind it, its telescope pointed towards the sky.

It’s the sort of place that could have wandered out of a cosy television romance. But beneath the familiar small-town charm, Cedar Hollow has a character entirely its own.

Maple Grove

In Cedar Hollow, Maine, Maple Grove brings together houses from different eras, each with a character of its own. Along its tree-lined pavement, an ivy-and-rose-covered cottage shares the street with a cream-painted Victorian, a shotgun house, and a red-brick Edwardian.

There’s a gentle rhythm to life here: neighbours tend their gardens, a boy reads an improbably large leather-bound book on a porch, and a shaggy-haired dog walker passes with a companion large enough to be mistaken for a wolf. Summer breezes carry birdsong, flowers, and the scent of baking through the street.

Agnes Hart’s house

Number 5 Maple Grove is the red-brick Edwardian Oliver Hart inherits from his great-aunt Agnes. Time has weathered its exterior: paint peels from the white window frames, the garden has grown wild, and the sun-faded porch creaks beneath visitors’ feet.

Inside, the house holds the traces of a full and sociable life. Photographs crowd the hall, showing Agnes among neighbours at barbecues, summer fairs, book-club gatherings, Christmas celebrations, and Halloween festivities.

Coats still hang beside a crowded umbrella stand, dust settles on the stair banisters, and sunlight catches the quiet kitchen. In the spacious living room, furniture waits beneath old white sheets. For all its neglect, the house still feels like a home that was deeply loved.

Local favourites

The Olive and Adder

With red leather booths, a chequered floor, and a jukebox in the corner, the Olive and Adder offers comfort with a Greek influence. Its handwritten menus feature English and Greek, while the counter tempts visitors with pies, baklava, cinnamon rolls, and cake. Come hungry—and be polite to your waitress.

Holloway’s Convenience Store

Henry Holloway’s shop turns a simple grocery run into a small adventure. Narrow aisles wind between generously stocked shelves, where everyday essentials sit in unexpected company. Fortunately, Henry is usually ready to point a bewildered shopper towards whatever they need.

Second Chances

Maribel Finch’s antique shop and thrift emporium is a treasure hunt through Cedar Hollow’s past. Pocket watches, mismatched china, old books, and well-loved furniture crowd its winding pathways. Maribel has a sharp eye for an object’s history—and for the possibilities its next owner might see.

The town square

Old maple trees shade the benches, flowers brighten the beds, and a fountain provides a gentle soundtrack to town life. Overlooking it all stands the pale-stone town hall, crowned by a copper-roofed clock tower. The community noticeboard offers a glimpse of local priorities, from bake sales to missing pets.

Twilight Books

Behind its dark green frontage, Twilight Books offers shelves of stories, golden lamplight, and plush armchairs made for lingering. It’s a welcoming little retreat for anyone who would rather spend an evening between the pages of a book.

Feather Flicks

Cedar Hollow’s old-fashioned cinema brings velvet seats, popcorn, and a little big-screen excitement to small-town life. Whether you prefer a quiet evening or a horror film, there’s something appealing about escaping into someone else’s story for a while.

Meet the neighbours

Oliver Hart — The newcomer

An inherited house brings Oliver to Cedar Hollow, but settling in proves rather less straightforward than unpacking his truck. Curious, quick with a joke, and determined to find a sensible explanation for almost anything, he approaches his new neighbours with a mixture of bewilderment and warmth. He’s looking for a fresh start—even if he isn’t quite sure what that should look like.

Noah Carter — A welcoming face

Usually found behind the café counter or helping out at Twilight Books, Noah has an easy smile and a willingness to lend a hand. He’s better at making people feel welcome than producing recognisable latte art, though he keeps trying. Between work, friendly teasing, and offers of practical help, he makes Cedar Hollow feel a little less unfamiliar.

Agnes Hart — A neighbour remembered

Oliver’s great-aunt left behind more than a house full of belongings. Across Cedar Hollow, neighbours remember her kindness, her baking, and her habit of helping whenever someone needed it. From community gatherings to small everyday favours, Agnes made herself part of the town’s life—and left traces of that life throughout Number 5 Maple Grove.

Daniel Blackwood — The bookseller

The man behind Twilight Books is a quiet presence on Maple Grove, with a reserved manner that gives way to gentle humour. Among the shelves and comfortable chairs of his shop, there’s a warmth that newcomers might not immediately expect. Like any good bookseller, Daniel offers a reason to look beyond first impressions.

Lucian Cross — The impeccably dressed lawyer

Polished, charming, and rarely caught without the right words, Lucian handles Agnes’s estate and welcomes Oliver to his new home. He’s also a Maple Grove neighbour who offers help with the same composure he brings to legal matters. His immaculate suits and measured manners make him a distinctive figure even in Cedar Hollow.

Margaret Ashcombe — The mayor with muddy boots

Margaret moves comfortably between town-hall paperwork and work in the garden. Warm, practical, and armed with a knowing sense of humour, she takes a lively interest in her neighbours. Keeping Cedar Hollow running means knowing its people—and Margaret seems to know just who needs a task, a welcome, or a little encouragement.

Maribel Finch — An eye for second chances

At her antique shop, Maribel sees possibilities in things other people are ready to discard. Curious and sharp-eyed, she moves through her crowded collection with the confidence of someone who knows exactly what she’s looking for. Old furniture, unusual ornaments, and visiting customers all receive her enthusiastic attention.
